**Setup:**
- Cantilever: L=30 m, h=1 m, fixed at x=0, free at x=30
- Plane stress, E=2×10¹¹ Pa, ν=0
- Right end: uniform upward traction σ_xy=28000 Pa over face area 1 m² → total shear force P=28000 N, no applied moment (uniform distribution, resultant at centroid)
- Probes at mid-height y=0.5 (neutral axis), so Dy equals the beam deflection w(x)

**Analysis:**

With ν=0, plane stress gives simple Hooke's law (σ=Eε, no coupling). The classical 2D Airy stress function solution for a cantilever with end load yields the centerline deflection exactly equal to the Euler-Bernoulli result:

w(x) = P/(6EI) × (3Lx² − x³)

where I = bh³/12 = 1×1³/12 = 1/12 m⁴, so 6EI = 6×2×10¹¹×(1/12) = 10¹¹

P/(6EI) = 28000/10¹¹ = 2.8×10⁻⁷

The Saint-Venant corrections from the actual BCs (displacement condition at fixed end vs. stress condition; uniform vs. parabolic shear at free end) decay as exp(−πd/h) where d is distance from the boundary. At the nearest probes (2.5h from each end), corrections are ~10⁻⁹ m — negligible. The fine mesh (240×40, cubic interpolation) with probes landing exactly on grid points means negligible discretization error.
